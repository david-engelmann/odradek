# Contract - odradek

This contract is the executable spec for odradek. Every obligation below is stated so it can fail. The full contract is the first build act of this repo and it extends the stub obligations written here.

## Stub obligations

ZH-S1. Every suspect on the hunt list carries the evidence that it serves nobody, and a suspect listed without evidence is a defect the tests must catch.

ZH-S2. No kill fires before its proof is recorded, and an action taken on a suspect whose proof is missing or failed is a defect the tests must catch.

ZH-S3. The first action on any proven zombie is a reversible quarantine, and an irreversible action as the first step on any suspect is forbidden by the contract.

ZH-S4. Every hunt works from recorded or synthetic cluster state during the first slices, and no code path in those slices connects to a live cluster.

ZH-S5. Every fixture and example in this repo is synthetic and built for this repo, and no real trace or session data appears anywhere in the tree.

## Round 1 obligations

Round 1 works over one recorded cluster snapshot. A meaningful line is a line that is neither blank nor a comment, and a comment line is a line whose first character that is not a space is the hash character. A snapshot opens with one header line of the form snapshot followed by the snapshot time as a whole number of seconds on the snapshot clock. After the header come entry blocks in any order. An entry block opens with the word entry followed by the entry identifier as one token, and then carries exactly one line for each of these fields, in any order after the opening line. The kind field names process or run. The starter field names what started the entry as one token. The state field names running or finished. The signal interval field names a positive whole number of seconds. The last signal field names a whole number of seconds on the snapshot clock or the word none when no heartbeat or progress signal is recorded. The holds field names a comma separated list of resource tokens with no spaces or the word none when the entry holds nothing. The owner field names the identifier of the entry that owns this one or the word none when no owner is recorded. The token in-flight inside a holds list is the in-flight marker. Times are whole seconds on one stated clock so that every age the scan computes is an integer subtraction and no date parsing stands between the recording and the verdict.

Three proof rules decide every verdict, and each rule returns exactly one of satisfied, contradicted, or unknown for each entry. The signal rule is satisfied when the age of the last signal, snapshot time minus last signal, is at least three times the entry signal interval, contradicted when a signal is recorded and the age is under that threshold, and unknown when no signal is recorded. The owner rule is satisfied when the owner reference resolves to an entry recorded finished or to an identifier no entry in the snapshot carries, contradicted when it resolves to an entry recorded running, and unknown when no owner reference is recorded. The in-flight rule is satisfied when the holds list carries no in-flight marker and contradicted when it carries one, and it is never unknown because the holds field is always recorded. An entry recorded finished is settled and takes no verdict. A running entry whose signal age is under one signal interval is live. A running entry that satisfies all three proof rules is a proven zombie. Every other running entry is a suspect, and no suspect is ever reported as a zombie.

The scan prints a counts line naming the live, suspect, proven, and settled counts, then one block for every proven zombie and every suspect, sorted first by classification with proven zombies before suspects and then by identifier. Every block carries exactly three evidence lines, one per proof rule, a satisfied rule printed as an evidence for line, a contradicted rule printed as an evidence against line, and an unknown rule printed as a missing line, each line naming the recorded fact the verdict rests on. Resource tokens in a holds list print sorted so the output does not depend on the order the recording wrote them in.

The plan command writes a quarantine plan for one proven zombie and nothing else. The plan names the entry, restates the three evidence for lines of its proof, lists its steps with every step marked reversible, states an observation window of ten times the entry signal interval in seconds, states the escalation condition as a kill proposal drafted in a later round if the entry shows no signal for the whole window and no owner comes forward, and states that the tool executes nothing. Asking for a plan for an identifier the snapshot does not carry, or for an entry classified live, suspect, or settled, is refused with the identifier and the classification named.

ZH-1. A snapshot that breaks the recorded format is refused with an error that names the source and the defect, and the refusal covers a missing or duplicated header, an entry block before the header, an unknown field line, a duplicated or missing field inside a block, a duplicated entry identifier, a non positive signal interval, a last signal later than the snapshot time, and an unknown kind or state token, and no refusal ever surfaces as a traceback or as a partial document.

ZH-2. The signal rule is satisfied only when the recorded signal age is at least three times the entry signal interval, and an entry whose age falls one second short of that threshold is not satisfied, and an entry with no recorded signal returns unknown for this rule and can never be a proven zombie on the strength of the other two rules.

ZH-3. The owner rule is satisfied when the owner reference resolves to a finished entry or to an identifier absent from the snapshot, contradicted when it resolves to a running entry, and unknown when no owner reference is recorded, and an entry whose owner is recorded running is never a proven zombie.

ZH-4. The in-flight rule is contradicted when the recorded holds list carries the in-flight marker token and satisfied otherwise, and an entry holding the marker is never a proven zombie however stale its signal and however settled its owner.

ZH-5. A running entry with a signal age under one signal interval is classified live and is never listed, a running entry satisfying all three proof rules is classified proven zombie, every other running entry is classified suspect, and an entry recorded finished is classified settled and is never listed, and the classification of every entry is decided by these rules and no others.

ZH-6. The scan output lists every suspect and every proven zombie exactly once, sorted with proven zombies before suspects and by identifier inside each class, and it carries a counts line whose four counts equal the numbers of entries in each classification, and a snapshot with no suspects and no proven zombies prints the counts line alone.

ZH-7. Every listed entry carries exactly three evidence lines, one for each proof rule, with a satisfied rule printed as evidence for, a contradicted rule printed as evidence against, and an unknown rule printed as missing, and each evidence line names the recorded fact behind the verdict, so a listed entry without evidence cannot be printed.

ZH-8. The scan and the plan are pure functions of the snapshot text, so repeated runs over the same snapshot produce byte identical output, and shuffling the entry blocks or the tokens inside a holds list changes nothing in either document.

ZH-9. A plan asked for an identifier the snapshot does not carry is refused naming the identifier and its absence, and a plan asked for an entry classified live, suspect, or settled is refused naming the identifier and its classification, and each refusal is one error line with exit code 2 and no document on the output stream.

ZH-10. The plan for a proven zombie names the entry, restates the three evidence for lines of its proof, marks every step reversible, states the observation window as ten times the entry signal interval, states the escalation condition as a later round drafting a kill proposal, and states that the tool executes nothing, and a plan missing any of those parts is a defect.

ZH-11. The command line tool exits with code 0 when the scan or the plan document is produced and with code 2 when any input is refused, and a refusal prints one error line to the error stream and never a traceback, and the tool writes nothing to the standard output but the document it was asked for.

ZH-12. The tool reads only the snapshot file it is handed and prints documents, and no code path in this round connects to a live cluster, opens a network connection, or changes any input file, and producing a plan never performs the quarantine it describes.

ZH-13. The scan document opens with a header line naming the snapshot time it was computed from, written as the word snapshot followed by the recorded time, and the counts line and the entry blocks follow it in the order ZH-6 names.

## Round 2 obligations

Round 2 adopts the kill call the roadmap names, as an offline seam inside the plan and nowhere else. The scan never consults a judgment source. The plan consults one only for the proven zombie it names, only after the refusals of ZH-9 have had their say, and only from recorded fixtures in this round. A judgment answer can record a verdict on the plan and can advance the plan for operator approval, and it can do nothing else, and advancing a plan performs no part of it and approves nothing by itself.

ZH-14. The kill call reads one request built from the completed proof pack of one proven zombie and from nothing else. The request carries exactly four fields. The entry field names the entry identifier. The signal field, the owner field, and the in-flight field each carry exactly two fields, an outcome field naming that proof rule outcome for the entry as one of satisfied, contradicted, or unknown, and an evidence field naming that proof rule fact for the entry, copied word for word from the verdict the scan computed. The request is built by one builder from the verdict alone, so the same snapshot always builds the same request. A request naming a live entry, a suspect, a settled entry, or an absent identifier is never built, because the plan refuses those cases before any request exists.

ZH-15. A kill call judgment is recorded as one fixture pairing one request with one response. The fixture envelope carries exactly the fields synthetic, judgment_point, request, and response, the judgment_point field names the kill-call point, and the synthetic field is true, and a fixture carrying any other top level field or missing one of these is refused at load with the defect named. A response of the choice kind carries exactly the fields kind, choice, confidence, and probabilities, and a response of the abstention kind carries exactly the field kind and nothing else. The choice field names one of the three labels quarantine, watch, and spare. The probabilities field carries a number for each of the three labels and for no other label, each number between 0 and 1, and the three numbers add up to 1 within a tolerance of one thousandth. The confidence field is a number between 0 and 1. A response kind that is not a string is a malformed answer, and a numeric field whose value cannot be converted to a finite float is not a number for these checks, and an answer breaking any part of this shape is refused at load when it arrives in a fixture and counts as no judgment when it arrives from any other source.

ZH-16. The fixture player loads one directory of fixture files and validates every pair as it loads, and two fixtures recording the same request refuse the load, and a match is exact on the canonical JSON of the request, and where no fixture records the request the player abstains and returns no answer. The player reads only the directory it is handed and never reaches the network. A fixtures directory that cannot be loaded, because it is missing or unreadable or holds a fixture outside the shape ZH-15 states, refuses the plan command with one error line and exit code 2 and no traceback and no document on the output stream.

ZH-17. A judgment answer is trusted only when it is well formed for the kill call under ZH-15 and its confidence reaches 0.6, and an answer at exactly 0.6 is trusted and an answer under 0.6 is not. Before any answer is trusted it passes the same shape check the fixture player applies at load, whoever handed the answer back. An abstention, an answer under the threshold, an answer outside the shape, and no source at all each count as no judgment, and where no judgment stands the plan is the plan Round 1 wrote.

ZH-18. The plan command accepts a fixtures option naming a fixtures directory, and the directory is loaded before the plan is written, so a directory that cannot be loaded refuses the command on the terms ZH-16 states. The kill call is asked only inside the plan, only about the proven zombie the plan names, and only after the plan refusals of ZH-9, and a refused plan asks nothing. Where a trusted judgment stands, the plan records the verdict as two lines after the escalation line and before the closing line that states the tool executes nothing. The first verdict line is the words kill call verdict, then the label the judgment chose, then the words with confidence, then the confidence printed in the shortest decimal form that round trips to the recorded value. The second verdict line states the consequence. For a quarantine verdict the consequence line reads verdict advances this plan, the plan is ready for an operator to approve, and the approval itself stays an operator decision the tool never takes. For a watch verdict or a spare verdict the consequence line reads verdict leaves this plan unadvanced, no action follows from this answer, and the plan stands as written for an operator to read. Where no trusted judgment stands, the printed plan is byte identical to the plan Round 1 wrote for the same snapshot and identifier. A verdict never changes the classification line, the proof lines, the steps, the observation window, or the escalation line, and a plan carrying a verdict still closes by stating that the tool executes nothing.

ZH-19. The scan never consults a judgment source, and the scan document is byte identical whether the seam is present or not, and a plan written without the fixtures option never consults a judgment source either. No code path in this round connects to a live cluster or reaches a judgment provider, and every judgment the tool uses in this round comes from a recorded fixture in a directory it was handed.

## Round 3 obligations

Round 3 settles the readings of the holds list that the earlier rounds left unnamed, and it makes the one behaviour change the Round 3 probes forced. The probes run before this round was written found that a holds list carrying one token twice printed that token twice, once in the evidence lines and once in step 2 of the plan, because the parser kept the recorded tokens as written and the printers sorted them without deduplicating them. The list was sorted, as ZH-8 requires, and it was never a set. This round reads the list as a set at parse time, so the scan, the plan, and the kill call request all see each token once from one place, and no other reading of the snapshot format changes.

ZH-20. A holds list is read as a set of tokens, and the parser records each token once however many times the list writes it. A token repeated inside one holds list, the in-flight marker included, is the same recording as the token written once, and the scan document, the plan document, and the kill call request built over such a snapshot are byte identical to the same documents built over the snapshot with each token written once. That identity is the one behaviour change of this round, and it is confined to recordings that repeat a token inside one holds list. The holds field names resources and not entries, so an entry whose holds list carries its own identifier records nothing remarkable, and that token is inert unless it is the in-flight marker. A running entry may name itself as its owner, and the owner reference then resolves to a running entry, itself, so the owner rule is contradicted for that entry, and the entry is at most a suspect and never a proven zombie on that ground.

## Round 4 obligations

Round 4 reads the outcome of the watch the plan sets. The plan holds the quarantine for the observation window and watches for any signal, and its escalation line names what may follow a silent window, and until this round nothing in the tool read whether the window passed in silence. The observation report is that reading. It is deterministic and it consults no judgment source, and it performs nothing, and the scan and the plan and their documents do not change in this round.

ZH-21. The observe command takes the snapshot a plan was written from, a second snapshot recorded later, and the entry identifier the plan names, and it writes one observation report and nothing else. An observation asked over a second snapshot whose recorded time is not later than the earlier snapshot time is refused naming both recorded times. An observation asked for an identifier the earlier snapshot does not carry is refused naming the identifier and its absence, and an observation asked for an entry classified live, suspect, or settled in the earlier snapshot is refused naming the identifier and its earlier classification. Each refusal is one error line with exit code 2 and no document on the output stream and never a traceback.

ZH-22. The report opens with a line written as the words observation report for followed by the entry identifier, and then a line naming the earlier snapshot time after the words planned from snapshot, and a line naming the later snapshot time after the words observed at snapshot, and a line naming the elapsed seconds between the two times after the word elapsed and before the word seconds, and a line naming the observation window after the words observation window and before the word seconds. The window is ten times the signal interval the earlier entry records, computed from the earlier entry alone exactly as the plan computes it, so a signal interval the later snapshot records differently for the entry changes nothing in the report. Where the elapsed seconds fall short of the window the report gives the outcome line outcome watch still running and no verdict, and where the elapsed seconds reach the window the report gives exactly one verdict outcome line, outcome quarantine held or outcome quarantine broken.

ZH-23. The verdict reads the recorded facts of the later snapshot and nothing else. A sign of life is a last signal recorded in the later snapshot later than the last signal the earlier snapshot recorded, or an owner recorded running in the later snapshot under the owner rule the scan states. The quarantine broke when either sign of life stands, and it held when neither stands, and the entry absent from the later snapshot shows no sign of life. Any other recorded signal value, the same value, an earlier value, or no signal recorded at all, is no sign of life on the signal fact, and an owner in the later snapshot that is finished or absent or unrecorded is no sign of life on the owner fact. The verdict never reads the classification the scan gives the entry in the later snapshot, so an entry the later scan calls settled can still have broken the watch by signalling inside the window. The report states the evidence for its verdict as evidence lines in the printed style of the scan, two spaces in from the margin, a fact that supports the verdict printed as an evidence for line and a fact that broke the quarantine printed as an evidence against line, each line naming the recorded fact it read.

ZH-24. The observation rests on the proof that wrote the plan. A proven zombie satisfied all three proof rules in the earlier snapshot, so the earlier entry of a proven zombie always carries a recorded last signal, and the observe command reads that signal without refusing its absence. The report consults no judgment source and no fixture directory, and it is a pure function of the two snapshot texts, so repeated runs over the same pair of snapshots produce byte identical output. The report closes with the line executes nothing, this report is a document and the tool performs no part of it, and no code path in this round connects to a live cluster or changes any input file, and reading an observation never lifts or extends the quarantine it reports on.
